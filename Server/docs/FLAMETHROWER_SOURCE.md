# Army flamethrower source contract

The recovered 1.4.0 flamethrower is an infantry weapon on `SoldierBehaviourFlamethrower`, not a player inventory weapon. `WeaponFlamethrower` has weapon type 12, a serialized 0.5-second prefab cadence, infinite ammunition, and no reload. `SoldierBehaviour.ApplyWeaponsSetup` replaces that cadence with 0.35 seconds at runtime. Each real shot creates one `FlameAmmo` instance. The ammo performs exactly six independent overlap checks after strict 0.35-second intervals, then expires.

Each pulse queries a radius of 3 around the weapon spawn point. It ignores the owning side, ignores a collider-center distance below 0.3, flattens only the weapon's forward Y coordinate, and accepts an enemy when its center angle is strictly below `Lerp(60, 40, Pow(distance, 0.25))`. Damage uses `Lerp(effectiveDamage * 0.1 / 6, effectiveDamage / 6, 1 - distance / 3)`. The recovered behavior overwrites the prefab's serialized 8/30 damage values with these effective-unit values.

`ArmyFlameBurst` owns the six host pulse deadlines. At the fixed 30 Hz server clock, the source strict-greater-than test makes each interval eleven ticks, at offsets 11, 22, 33, 44, 55, and 66. `ArmyRusherAttackState` separately enforces the runtime weapon's strict 11-tick cadence between shots in one batch. The validated unit-upgrade catalog reads `REALSHOTPROBABILITY`, `BATCHSIZEMIN/MAX`, and `SHOTFREQUENCYMIN/MAX` from every recovered army stage and composes the selected normal, special, and elite lanes exactly as `UpgradeSlotsBaseSoldier.LoadData` adds them. Flamethrower stage zero therefore supplies probability 0.8 and batch 1/1. After the batch, `ReturnToPreviousStateFromShot` overrides the generic 2.3-3.0 stage timing with the Rusher-specific random 2-4 second cooldown. The flamethrower windup is exactly zero; this is pinned separately from ordinary and SWAT animation windups. A validated per-unit `ArmyAccuracyCoefficients` manifest value then scales the fully composed probability, matching the recovered `Perk.ScaleByPerk` call order; omission preserves coefficient 1. MatchEngine samples the current opposing animated collision model for every pulse, applies Flame damage through the common player authority, publishes shot/impact events, accounts each live burst under the global projectile limit, and aborts the unscored match if trusted collision or damage authority is damaged.

The focused `--flame-only` suite covers all pulse deadlines, the six-pulse terminal state, center-distance damage, minimum range, cone rejection, malformed authority, and batch cadence; the combat-content suite covers normal/special upgrade-row composition, accuracy scaling, manifest validation, and detached authority. The focused `--rusher-only` test now admits two real UDP peers, deploys a source Flamethrower with a trusted accuracy coefficient, observes host navigation, and requires an `army-flame` shot, pulse impact, and authoritative opponent health loss. This also corrected the shared Rusher intent contract to carry distinct owner and target player IDs; the earlier single-ID shape caused every live army shot to be discarded at its ownership gate. The combat manifest now pins the enemy rig gun-snap chain and every serialized Rusher weapon spawn-point chain. The host composes the flamethrower rest-pose muzzle offset and rotates it with authoritative target-facing instead of emitting pulses from the army root. Shoot-animation bone motion still needs a Unity-sampled pose export. Durable Backend entitlement derivation for the accuracy coefficient, unit visual/effect projection, and normal Client presentation remain part of B14/B32.

The `army-flame` shot event now carries that same host-composed weapon muzzle as its origin. A two-peer UDP check compares the event height with the moving unit's current authoritative root and the pinned muzzle offset. The six later damage pulses continue to recompute the muzzle from the unit's then-current position and facing, as the recovered `FlameAmmo` does.

Each pulse also queries the current animated collision parts of opposing deployed infantry using the same recovered radius, strict distance threshold, cone, and damage interpolation as player parts. The recovered `enemy.prefab` infantry damage parts serialize `shotCoeficient: 1`, so the computed Flame amount enters the shared army vitality/death transition without an additional factor; friendly units are excluded by their server-owned faction. The combat-content suite now deploys actual opposing Flamethrowers, places a deterministic host pulse against one sampled infantry part, verifies target vitality loss and owner exclusion, and rejects an absent firing entity. Natural movement in the two-peer Park test did not bring those units into the player-directed cone, so a normal live two-army hit and its presentation remain unverified.

The same source pulse now queries live cover-shield colliders through the recovered map overlap geometry. `Shield.DoDamage` applies `UnitToShieldCoef` only to `Shot`; `FlameAmmo` calls `Burn`, so the host applies raw Flame damage without the ordinary unit-bullet multiplier. A Park host test places a pulse at a pinned shield collider, observes both distinct enemy shields inside its three-unit sphere lose health, verifies the selected shield loses exactly the source cone damage, and confirms allied shields stay unchanged.

The four ground-vehicle prefabs each serialize a root `DestroyableObjectMultipleParts.shotCoeficient` of 0.4. Its `Awake` copies that value to each child damage part; the later `AICar.UpgradesLoaded` change to the root's 0.33 bullet coefficient does not refresh a child before `Burn` (only `Shoot` refreshes it). The version-9 vehicle artifact pins the Flame coefficient and each part transform position. A pulse tests the live body colliders against the recovered three-unit sphere, uses box/sphere collider centers but capsule transform origins for the cone, de-duplicates parts by vehicle root, and applies part weight times 0.4 to synchronized Army and vehicle-registry health. A deterministic deployed Humvee test verifies the amount, owner exclusion, rear-cone rejection, and shared-health agreement. The host processes source-sorted body parts deterministically; Unity `Physics.OverlapSphere` does not promise that ordering, so a multi-part overlap can choose a different first part.

The recovered Decoy prefab has one root `DestroyableObject` sharing its enabled BoxCollider and a serialized `shotCoeficient` of 1. The version-2 Decoy source artifact pins that component and value. Each Flame pulse tests current opposing Decoy box centers, applies source cone damage to the host Decoy registry, and on lethal damage removes drone-target authority, frees the exact obstacle reservation, and emits `DecoyDestroyed`. A deterministic match proof deploys the Flamethrower with both factions' Decoys, verifies the first pulse amount, repeated lethal damage, event publication, obstacle release, and allied Decoy exclusion.

The Heavy Turret prefab has one `DestroyableObjectMultipleParts` root with coefficient one and three child damage parts with weight one, each sharing a source BoxCollider. The version-5 Heavy Turret artifact pins those component bindings. A pulse queries the turret's current joint-rotated collider geometry, chooses one part per root, and routes the damage through the host turret health/event transition. A match with both factions' turrets confirms the exact first-pulse amount and allied exclusion. Unity's overlap ordering and natural in-match positioning remain unverified.

The Drone prefab's root `DestroyableObject` has coefficient one and shares the root BoxCollider. Its child sphere remains on a destroyable layer but has no `DestroyableObject`, so `FlameAmmo.CheckHit` cannot damage through that child. The pinned air-geometry inventory verifies the root component/box binding; `Drone.UpgradesLoaded` does not replace its coefficient. The host now queries the live Drone root box at its current rotation and applies the source Flame amount to shared army health. A deployed opposing Flamethrower/Drone match proves the first-pulse amount.

The Helicopter prefab's `DestroyableObjectMultipleParts` root serializes coefficient one and copies it into twelve child parts at `Awake`; eleven child boxes have the pinned active collision geometry. `Helicopter.UpgradesLoaded` later changes only the root to the 0.33 armored bullet coefficient, while child `Burn` does not refresh it. A Flame pulse now tests the current host-rotated body boxes and applies one coefficient-one child hit to shared Helicopter health. A deployed Flamethrower/Helicopter match verifies the amount and that the gunner's separate health does not change. Original Unity overlap ordering and natural in-match contact remain open.

The special-lane Transporter's `miniDrone.prefab` has a root `DestroyableObject` sharing its trigger BoxCollider, with serialized Flame coefficient one. Version 10 of the ground-vehicle artifact pins that damage component and value. Each Flame pulse tests the two active repair drones at their host positions and rotations, applies damage to their separate health, and on lethal damage removes their collision target and emits the existing down event. A deterministic opposing Flamethrower/Transporter match verifies one pulse and lethal cleanup. The recovered `DynamicsManager.asset` sets `m_QueriesHitTriggers: 1`, so the trigger participates in `Physics.OverlapSphere`. Natural in-match contact and remaining eligible targets remain unverified.

The recovered Flame loop requires a `DestroyableObject` on the exact collider GameObject and a non-null `DestroyableObject.owner` before its faction and cone checks. Scene barrels have only `PhotonView`, `PhotonLevelView`, `DestroyableObject`, and `Barrel` as their script components (for example Aztec source GameObject 266); `Barrel` does not implement `IFraction` and does not assign the damage component's owner. `DestroyableObject.Awake` therefore leaves a scene barrel's owner null, so the recovered Flame loop skips it. Do not add Flame-to-barrel damage merely because the general bullet/explosion paths support barrels. `MineAmmo.prefab` has no `DestroyableObject` on its trigger/root and is likewise not a Flame target. A `KillStreakBonusBox` does implement `IFraction` alongside its required damage component, making a live crate a possible Flame target; host crate lifecycle, exact prefab collider binding, and crate reward authority are still absent and need separate B14/B19 implementation and proof.
