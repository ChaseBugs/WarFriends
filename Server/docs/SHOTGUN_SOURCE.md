# Shotgun source package

The recovered 1.4.0 MainScene has nine player shotgun bindings and 464 upgrade rows: SPAS (36), Benelli (26), Saiga (46), Striker (56), Blackhand (76), SawnOff (56), StrikerElite (56), AA12 (66), and SaigaElite (46). `SelfHostedShotgunExport.Run` records the scene binding, inventory index, player controller, muzzle path, bullet prefab and `ShotgunLevelsSetup`/`ShotGunBulletSetup` values in `recovered-shotgun-bindings.json`. `shotgun-content-manifest.json` pins the scene, stats and export hashes. `ShotgunCatalog` requires the complete ordered package and provides stage damage, ammo, reload, rate, critical probability and player-damage ratios.

At runtime, `ShotgunLevelsSetup.SetUpWeapon` copies its `baseDefinition` to the pellet setup, then applies the selected `Google2u.Shotgun_*` upgrade row. Therefore the runtime SPAS cone is 50 degrees near and 3 degrees far with radius 10 and `BulletSlow` speed 30; the serialized setup's 35/8 degrees, radius 4.5 and speed 10 are stale. `Google2u.WeaponUpgrades.SPEED` is a display stat, not this projectile speed. All 18 serialized `ShotGunBulletSetup` components in MainScene have `fakeSpeedFactor: 1`, overriding the C# field initializer of 1.5; the nine exported player bindings now pin that value, so their fake speed equals the source runtime speed 30. Stage zero SPAS has 8 loaded and 18 reserve rounds, 4.3-second reload, 0.5-second rate of fire, and 22.62–101.8 base damage. These values must be selected by the server-authorized equipped level, never accepted as shot payloads.

`ShotgunShotPlanner` models the recovered `BulletShotGun.ShotRealAtPos`: one main real pellet on the aim direction; eligible extra destroyable colliders inside the distance-dependent cone; no more than two extra colliders per main object and seven extras total; damage linearly decreases from max at the muzzle to min at the radius; fake pellets fill the visual minimum of four and cannot deal damage. The source uses strict cone and 0.3-unit aim-center exclusion checks. The planner accepts host-owned collider candidates. `ShotgunVolleyFactory` turns its real selections into fast `BulletSlow` flights with one independent critical roll per pellet and server-owned damage coefficients. It does not insert those flights into a match or apply health changes.

`ShotgunFakePelletFlight` now reconstructs the separate visual-only branch: source `Random.onUnitSphere` spread rises to 0.25 units at a two-unit aim distance, fast `BulletSlow` clamps the first leg to 50 units, and its fake tween travels twice that displacement for twice the first-leg/fake-speed duration. Focused tests cover spread, tick progression, distance cap, and malformed inputs. The catalog pins the serialized fake-speed factor. Live `unscored-shotgun-combat` now prepares real and visual-only fake flights as one bounded batch, reserves ammo/projectile/event capacity atomically, and gives each fake an independent consecutive projectile ID. Fake spread uses two host random rolls per pellet to reconstruct a unit-sphere direction; exact Unity random-stream parity remains unproven. Fake flights never enter collision, health, hit credit, or reward authority. Snapshots and paged projectile scans expose `shotgun-bullet` and `shotgun-fake-bullet`, and the ordered shot-event stream includes the fake pellets.

`MatchEngine.ConfigureVisualVolley` validates the complete real/fake batch before inserting any pellet or spending a shell. It reserves projectile/event capacity for the whole volley, assigns consecutive projectile IDs, emits one shot event per pellet, and replays a retried click without repeating the volley. `ConfigureVolley` retains the previous real-only callback for other weapon families. The existing rifle callback uses the same path with a one-projectile batch.

The 142-node pose artifact already contains all nine shotgun muzzle paths. `PlayerPoseCatalog.AttachShotgunMuzzles` verifies each against the pinned shotgun binding and reconstructs its world transform from the same animated rig that drives player hitboxes. Tests confirm all nine move under the recovered shotgun firing clip and follow root placement. The separate scene-export `muzzlePosition` is a serialized reference: SPAS is at `(99.97178, 0.14078, 100.41551)` there, but its sampled T-pose is approximately `(99.73393, 0.16943, 99.34906)`. Do not use the serialized position as a live firing origin. A Unity shotgun-muzzle motion audit is still needed before claiming exact animated parity.

`ShotCollisionWorld.OverlapEnemy` derives shotgun candidates from the opposing player's current host pose and enemy Decoy, animated-infantry, ground-vehicle body, attached passenger, active repair mini-drone, Heavy Turret, Drone root, Helicopter body, and Helicopter gunner hitboxes in the live dynamic-target registry. Box, sphere and capsule volumes are tested against the muzzle-centered radius; using only collider centers would miss a collider whose near surface intersects the overlap. Only enabled, active parts on the shot's layer mask enter the bounded, stable candidate list. Player parts share the opponent's main-object identity; dynamic parts share their respective entity, passenger-role, or mini-drone-path identity. The ground-vehicle catalog explicitly marks its placed `DestroyableObjectpart` body colliders. The Heavy Turret's three child damage boxes and Helicopter's eleven body boxes have separate source-pinned `DestroyableObjectpart` graphs; the latter uses its original layer eight. The Drone's root box owns `DestroyableObject` and uses its runtime flying-faction layer; its unowned layer-eight child sphere is excluded from shotgun extras. The gunner's three sampled soldier parts share one identity on the opposing soldier layer. Repeated sampled collider paths receive deterministic ordinals so the planner can distinguish body and head while retaining the source two-extras-per-object limit. The query rejects the serialized reference pose. A live match test proves an activated Decoy contributes an extra real-pellet plan; focused tests prove infantry layer selection, a source-pinned Humvee body, mini-drone root, attached passenger, aimed Heavy Turret, and live deployed Drone/Helicopter bodies and Helicopter gunner with their layer and root limits. Existing shield and geometry raycasts still decide impact. Unity broadphase order across these colliders is not proven. Other destructible world objects and shield colliders need source ownership and layer-mask treatment before general B09 support.

The opt-in `unscored-shotgun-combat` mode now loads both pinned packages through `NetworkWorker`, verifies the full shotgun `WeaponManifest` against its upgrade row, and binds each player to the source animation family. Its first click follows the recovered cover delay; one accepted shot spends one shell, prepares the host-selected real pellets, advances their fast flights through current map/player collision, applies player damage and credits confirmed hits. An in-process City encounter observes health loss and hit credit after the defending cover's riot shield obstructs its idle pose. A two-client signed UDP encounter now also requires health loss and hit credit in the SDK snapshot; both peers receive identical ordered shot and impact events. The mode remains unscored and cannot grant rewards.

`PlayerController.SetupCollisionLayers` recursively assigns the ragdoll the fraction bullet layer (Enemies 23, Allies 22). The host now carries that trusted layer with each current player pose, so the recovered bullet mask gates player parts in both `Physics.OverlapSphere` reconstruction and projectile raycasts. Focused tests prove a layer-23 player is absent under a layer-22-only mask and present under layer 23. Unlayered test fixtures retain their former behavior; live rifle and grenade simulation provide the manifest fraction explicitly.

This is a live **player-and-shield** shotgun checkpoint with additional source-backed dynamic overlap planning, not complete B09 or a normal Unity battle. The Backend allocator still must prove loadout and shield-level ownership before issuing a manifest. Other dynamic world destroyables, card-activated shield parts, exact Unity muzzle motion parity, exact Unity fake-pellet random/tween parity, reload animation, normal Client spawn/input/render ownership, and all explosion/status variants remain open. With both shield levels signed into the manifest, a scene riot shield now loses durability, drops its collider at zero HP, and repairs after six seconds.

The [shield source catalog and lifecycle](SHIELD_SOURCE.md) provide the rank HP, collider and repair rules used by this opt-in live path.

Validation: Unity 2018.3.0f2 editor export reported `SHOTGUN_EXPORT_PASSED weapons=9`. The revised exporter returned factor one for all nine player bindings. Its disposable compile copy has modified BulletShotGun/BulletSlow prefab file hashes; excluding those two digest fields, the regenerated JSON equals the pinned package, and the active Client prefabs still match the package hashes. `War.Battle.Tests` checks the complete 464-stage catalog and the pellet planner alongside the live rifle UDP suite.

The normal Client projectile presenter accepts both shotgun snapshot kinds and copies the recovered `BulletSlow` pellet mesh and `LineTrailRenderer` parameters from an owned shotgun without gameplay components. A Unity 2018 Editor audit verifies both visuals, mesh identity, trail attachment, complete/truncated snapshot removal and absence of local collision authority. A signed two-peer UDP check verifies the fake shot events, while focused host tests verify no fake damage. Every shotgun projectile row now carries the exact server-authored `Google2u.Shotgun_*` source ID. The portable SDK accepts only the nine recovered IDs for these rows, and the Unity presenter resolves that ID against the nine pinned `MainScene` inventory indexes, so a later weapon switch cannot change an in-flight pellet’s mesh or trail setup. The Edit Mode audit also distinguishes a Benelli trail from SPAS while both pellet kinds remain visible. The audit is an Edit Mode presentation check, not two-device Play Mode proof.
