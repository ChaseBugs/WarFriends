# Shotgun source package

The recovered 1.4.0 MainScene has nine player shotgun bindings and 464 upgrade rows: SPAS (36), Benelli (26), Saiga (46), Striker (56), Blackhand (76), SawnOff (56), StrikerElite (56), AA12 (66), and SaigaElite (46). `SelfHostedShotgunExport.Run` records the scene binding, inventory index, player controller, muzzle path, bullet prefab and `ShotgunLevelsSetup`/`ShotGunBulletSetup` values in `recovered-shotgun-bindings.json`. `shotgun-content-manifest.json` pins the scene, stats and export hashes. `ShotgunCatalog` requires the complete ordered package and provides stage damage, ammo, reload, rate, critical probability and player-damage ratios.

At runtime, `ShotgunLevelsSetup.SetUpWeapon` copies its `baseDefinition` to the pellet setup, then applies the selected `Google2u.Shotgun_*` upgrade row. Therefore the runtime SPAS cone is 50 degrees near and 3 degrees far with radius 10 and `BulletSlow` speed 30; the serialized setup's 35/8 degrees, radius 4.5 and speed 10 are stale. `Google2u.WeaponUpgrades.SPEED` is a display stat, not this projectile speed. Stage zero SPAS has 8 loaded and 18 reserve rounds, 4.3-second reload, 0.5-second rate of fire, and 22.62–101.8 base damage. These values must be selected by the server-authorized equipped level, never accepted as shot payloads.

`ShotgunShotPlanner` models the recovered `BulletShotGun.ShotRealAtPos`: one main real pellet on the aim direction; eligible extra destroyable colliders inside the distance-dependent cone; no more than two extra colliders per main object and seven extras total; damage linearly decreases from max at the muzzle to min at the radius; fake pellets fill the visual minimum of four and cannot deal damage. The source uses strict cone and 0.3-unit aim-center exclusion checks. The planner accepts host-owned collider candidates. `ShotgunVolleyFactory` turns its real selections into fast `BulletSlow` flights with one independent critical roll per pellet and server-owned damage coefficients. It does not insert those flights into a match or apply health changes.

`MatchEngine.ConfigureVolley` now validates a bounded host-prepared batch before inserting any pellet or spending a shell. It reserves projectile/event capacity for the whole volley, assigns consecutive projectile IDs, emits one shot event per real pellet, and replays a retried click without repeating the volley. The existing rifle callback uses the same path with a one-projectile batch.

The 142-node pose artifact already contains all nine shotgun muzzle paths. `PlayerPoseCatalog.AttachShotgunMuzzles` verifies each against the pinned shotgun binding and reconstructs its world transform from the same animated rig that drives player hitboxes. Tests confirm all nine move under the recovered shotgun firing clip and follow root placement. The separate scene-export `muzzlePosition` is a serialized reference: SPAS is at `(99.97178, 0.14078, 100.41551)` there, but its sampled T-pose is approximately `(99.73393, 0.16943, 99.34906)`. Do not use the serialized position as a live firing origin. A Unity shotgun-muzzle motion audit is still needed before claiming exact animated parity.

`ShotCollisionWorld.OverlapEnemy` now derives shotgun candidates from the opposing player's current host pose. Box, sphere and capsule volumes are tested against the muzzle-centered radius; using only collider centers would miss a collider whose near surface intersects the overlap. Only enabled, active parts enter the source-order candidate list, and each part gets a bounded stable identity while both parts share the opponent's main-object identity. The query rejects the serialized reference pose. This is player-only overlap authority; destructible world objects, units, vehicles and shield colliders need their own current-state enumeration and layer-mask treatment before general B09 support.

The opt-in `unscored-shotgun-combat` mode now loads both pinned packages through `NetworkWorker`, verifies the full shotgun `WeaponManifest` against its upgrade row, and binds each player to the source animation family. Its first click follows the recovered cover delay; one accepted shot spends one shell, prepares the host-selected real pellets, advances their fast flights through current map/player collision, applies player damage and credits confirmed hits. An in-process City encounter observes health loss and hit credit after the defending cover's riot shield obstructs its idle pose. A two-client signed UDP encounter now also requires health loss and hit credit in the SDK snapshot; both peers receive identical ordered shot and impact events. The mode remains unscored and cannot grant rewards.

This is a live **player-and-shield** shotgun checkpoint, not complete B09 or a normal Unity battle. The Backend allocator still must prove loadout and shield-level ownership before issuing a manifest. Dynamic world destroyables, units, vehicles, card-activated shield parts, exact Unity muzzle motion parity, fake-pellet presentation, reload animation, normal Client spawn/input/render ownership, and all explosion/status variants remain open. With both shield levels signed into the manifest, a scene riot shield now loses durability, drops its collider at zero HP, and repairs after six seconds.

The [shield source catalog and lifecycle](SHIELD_SOURCE.md) provide the rank HP, collider and repair rules used by this opt-in live path.

Validation: Unity 2018.3.0f2 editor export reported `SHOTGUN_EXPORT_PASSED weapons=9`; `War.Battle.Tests` checks the complete 464-stage catalog and the pellet planner alongside the live rifle UDP suite.
